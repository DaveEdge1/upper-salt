          Where everything is

          The Salt forms where the Black and White Rivers meet, upstream of the Highway 60 put-in. Snow on the White Mountains feeds the spring runoff. Hover over (or tap) a gauge or snow station to see its last 7 days.

          Snowpack

          The 3-week and 3-month outlooks above come from the NWS Colorado Basin River Forecast Center. See their ensemble forecast for this gauge. From January through May they also publish a seasonal runoff volume forecast: CBRFC water supply forecast for SLCA3.

          Only small lakes sit above the put-in, so no dam controls flows in the Salt River Canyon. The White Mountains headwaters have small reservoirs and lakes, such as Big Lake, Sunrise Lake, Hawley Lake and Horseshoe Cienega Lake. They are mainly fishing and recreation lakes, hold little next to a spring runoff, and are not operated to release water for boating. What you float is mostly rain and snowmelt passing through.

          The nearest large dam is Roosevelt, at the bottom of the run, so its level matters only if you paddle on past the Highway 288 take-out onto the lake.

          Salt River Project operates Roosevelt and the other Salt and Verde reservoirs. SRP daily water report.
